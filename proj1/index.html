Project 1- Olivia Huang


Overview

In this project, we auto-aligned images of glass plate photographs from the Prokudin-Gorskii collection. Each panel represented R, G, B and their color intensities. We split the original panel into three separate images, then aligned them on top of each other to produce a full color image.


Approach

We split the panel evenly into three color channels. In order to align the images, two image comparing metrics were used: sum of squared differences and normalized cross correlation. The images in this page were generated by finding the displacement with the largest cross correlation. On small JPEG images, I searched over a [-15, 15] pixel displacement. This gets costly on large TIFF files, as calculating NCC and SSD both require summing over the entire image matrix for each displacement. So, an image pyramid was implemented, in which the image was resized to a 64x64 image. After finding the optimal shift for this size, the image was recursively sized up and searched within a 2 pixel margin until the image was at its original size.


Problems encountered

On a low resolution image such as the base 64x64 case, it was common for the algorithm to output a 0,0 shift as the resolution was too low to detect the small differences in detail. This forced me to expand the base case, so that more alignments could be searched on a finer level.

Bells and Whistles: Auto-Cropping

I implemented auto-cropping by averaging the column/rows at each end of the array. The borders tend to be drastic in color, therefore a color of .8 or more would indicate that there was a white border, and one with less than .2 meant a black border. I would then crop those borders.
